Six recipes from the book

A first taste of Recettes de Famille et Saveurs du Monde — six dishes drawn from four kitchens, from Mami Belfort's notebook to a Lebanese friendship.

Serves 3–4Preparation 20 minutes

Ingredients

  • 300 g romaine lettuce
  • 300 g tomatoes (about 3 medium)
  • 200 g baby cucumber (Lebanese or Persian)
  • 150 g red pepper (2 medium)
  • 100 g radishes (about 5–6)
  • 2–3 spring onions (stalks)
  • 20 g fresh mint
  • 2 thin Lebanese pita breads
  • 60 ml olive oil, plus extra for brushing the pitas
  • 60 ml fresh lemon juice
  • 1½ teaspoons sumac
  • 1 tablespoon pomegranate molasses
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • ¼ teaspoon pepper

Utensils

  • A large salad bowl
  • A chef's knife
  • A chopping board
  • A small bowl for the dressing

Method

Preheat the oven to 200 °C. Split the pita breads open and tear them into uneven pieces of about 4 cm. Brush lightly with olive oil and sprinkle with salt, pepper and ½ teaspoon of sumac. Spread on a baking tray and toast in the oven for 5–7 minutes, until golden and crisp. Set aside.

Cut the romaine lettuce into 2–3 cm ribbons. Cut the tomatoes into pieces of about 1 cm. Slice the cucumber into half-moons about ½ cm thick. Deseed the pepper and cut it into thin rings. Slice the radishes thinly. Slice the spring onions into rounds. Pull the mint leaves from their stalks and chop them roughly.

Make the dressing: in a small bowl, mix the lemon juice, the pomegranate molasses, the remaining sumac (1 teaspoon), the pepper and the olive oil. Taste and adjust the seasoning to your liking.

Put all the vegetables and herbs in the large salad bowl and pour over the dressing. Toss gently so everything is well coated. Just before serving, add the toasted pita pieces and toss quickly — they should stay crisp. Serve immediately.

Notes

The secret of fattoush lies in the freshness of the ingredients and in the timing: the herbs should be lively, the vegetables crunchy, and above all the toasted pitas must be added only at the last moment so they keep their crunch.

AllergensGluten (pita bread); may contain sulphites (pomegranate molasses) — none of the other 14 major allergens.

Nutritional valuesWhole recipe (~1 kg of salad with pitas): ~1,420 kcal, ~24 g protein, ~142 g carbohydrate, ~79 g fat — that is, per serving (3 people) about 473 kcal, 8 g protein, 47 g carbohydrate, 26 g fat; per serving (4 people) about 355 kcal, 6 g protein, 36 g carbohydrate, 20 g fat.

Dietary suitabilityVegetarian, vegan, halal and kosher, subject to the certification of the processed ingredients (pita bread and pomegranate molasses).

Serves 4 or 5Preparation 20 minutesCooking 20–25 minutes

The mixture can be made ahead, but only put it in to bake when you are ready to sit down.

Ingredients

  • 30 g plain flour, potato starch or cornflour
  • 150 g butter
  • 500 ml milk
  • 5 eggs
  • 150 g grated Emmental, Gruyère or Comté
  • Salt, pepper and 1 pinch of nutmeg

Utensils

  • A soufflé dish
  • A saucepan
  • A whisk
  • A bowl for separating the eggs
  • A wooden spoon

Method

Melt the butter, broken into pieces, in a saucepan, then take it off the heat and stir in the flour until smooth.

Meanwhile, heat the milk. Once it boils, pour it little by little onto the flour, stirring constantly so the mixture stays perfectly smooth. Return to the heat and cook, still stirring. When the mixture is thick and smooth, take the pan off the heat and turn the oven on to gas mark 7 (about 210 °C).

Separate the yolks from the whites. Beat the whites to very stiff peaks (add a grain of salt to help). Stir the yolks into the cooled mixture one at a time, beating energetically. Add the cheese.

Then fold in the beaten whites, lifting the mixture as you go. No lumps of the base should remain, but some white should still show so the soufflé rises well.

Butter the base and sides of the dish generously and dust it with flour.

Pour in the mixture and put it straight into the oven for 20 to 25 minutes.

Do not open the oven door. After 15 minutes you can lower the heat a little if it is browning too quickly. As soon as the soufflé is cooked, turn the oven off and open the door a crack, then open it further minute by minute.

AllergensGluten (flour) — Eggs — Milk (500 ml plus 150 g cheese) — none of the other 14 major allergens.

Nutritional valuesWhole recipe: ~2,488 kcal, ~95 g protein, ~52 g carbohydrate, ~218 g fat — that is, per serving (4 people) about 622 kcal, 24 g protein, 13 g carbohydrate, 55 g fat; per serving (5 people) about 498 kcal, 19 g protein, 10 g carbohydrate, 44 g fat.

Dietary suitabilityVegetarian, halal and kosher, subject to the certification of the processed ingredients (butter and cheese).

Serves 4Preparation 20 minutesCooking 1 hour

Ingredients

  • 200 g Arborio rice
  • 1 litre hot chicken stock
  • 1 sachet of saffron
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 100 ml dry white wine
  • 1 shallot, finely chopped
  • 60 g cold butter
  • 80 g freshly grated Parmesan
  • Salt, pepper

Utensils

  • A large saucepan for the stock, kept hot
  • A large saucepan for the risotto
  • A wooden spoon
  • A ladle
  • A mortar and pestle, for the saffron
  • A knife and chopping board

Method

Pour the chicken stock into a saucepan and keep it hot over a low heat. Using a mortar and pestle, grind the saffron to a fine powder, then stir it into the stock so it releases all its colour and scent.

In a large saucepan, heat the olive oil over a medium heat. Add the shallot and soften it for 3 to 4 minutes without letting it colour. Add the rice and stir for 2 to 3 minutes, until every grain is coated in oil and turns slightly translucent.

Add the white wine and let it reduce almost completely, stirring as it goes.

Now start adding the hot stock, one ladleful at a time. Wait until the liquid is almost entirely absorbed before adding the next, stirring regularly. Carry on until the rice is tender but still slightly firm in the centre. This takes about 45 to 50 minutes.

Take the pan off the heat. Add the cold butter in pieces and the grated Parmesan, then beat vigorously until the risotto is creamy and glossy. Taste and adjust with salt and pepper if needed.

Serve immediately, with extra freshly grated Parmesan if you like.

AllergensMilk (60 g butter, 80 g Parmesan) — Sulphites (white wine) — Possible gluten, depending on the brand of shop-bought chicken stock — none of the other 14 major allergens.

Nutritional valuesWhole recipe: ~1,569 kcal, ~55 g protein, ~75 g carbohydrate, ~107 g fat — that is, per serving (4 people) about 392 kcal, 14 g protein, 19 g carbohydrate, 27 g fat.

Dietary suitabilityKosher, subject to the certification of the processed ingredients (stock, butter, Parmesan).

Serves 4Preparation 30 minutesCooking 3 hours

Ingredients

  • 800 g lamb shoulder, in pieces
  • 670 g onions
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • Salt
  • 2 teaspoons cumin seeds
  • ⅔ teaspoon ground ginger
  • ⅔ teaspoon coriander seeds
  • ⅓ teaspoon mild chilli
  • 1.5 g saffron
  • 1 to 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 to 2 preserved lemons
  • The juice of ½ to 1 lemon
  • 65 g black olives

Utensils

  • A mortar and pestle
  • A tagine dish, or a tagine pot with a conical lid
  • A knife and chopping board

Method

In a mortar, crush the garlic and all the spices together.

In a tagine dish, arrange the pieces of meat, cut into quarters, and the preserved lemons, then pour over the olive oil and the lemon juice. Scatter over the crushed garlic and spices and season with salt. Cover. Bring to the heat and cook very gently for at least 3 hours, counting from the moment it comes to the boil.

In the remaining oil, soften the sliced onions; they should not take on any colour. Once they have melted right down, tip them over the meat with the black olives, 15 minutes before the end of the cooking.

Serve with rice.

AllergensNone of the 14 major allergens identified.

Nutritional valuesWhole recipe: ~2,189 kcal, ~178 g protein, ~87 g carbohydrate, ~113 g fat — that is, per serving (4 people) about 547 kcal, 45 g protein, 22 g carbohydrate, 28 g fat.

Dietary suitabilityHalal and kosher, subject to the certification of the lamb.

Serves 6Preparation 1 hr 30Cooking 45 minutes to 1 hour

Ingredients

  • 300 g shortcrust pastry, or 300 g puff pastry
  • 90 g butter
  • 1.4 kg Granny Smith apples, peeled and quartered
  • 100 g sugar
  • Crème fraîche, to serve

Utensils

  • A tarte Tatin tin, or a round tin that can go on the hob
  • A rolling pin
  • A cooling rack
  • A serving plate

Method

Preheat the oven to 190 °C.

Melt the butter in the tin, sprinkle over half the sugar, arrange the apple quarters inside and sprinkle over the remaining sugar.

Put the tin straight onto the hob to start the sugar caramelising.

Cook for about 15 minutes, until it takes on a pale golden colour.

Roll the pastry out into a circle larger than the tin. Cover the apples with it and tuck it in around them, then bake for about 20 minutes to brown the pastry. Take the tart out and let it rest for about 15 to 20 minutes before turning it out.

Put the tin back on the hob for a few minutes to soften the caramel, then turn the tart out onto a serving plate.

Serve the tarte Tatin warm, with crème fraîche.

Variations

You can vary this recipe in several ways: increase the butter or the sugar, use puff pastry instead of shortcrust, or replace the apples with other fruit (pears, mangoes).

AllergensGluten (shortcrust or puff pastry) — Milk (90 g butter, crème fraîche to serve) — none of the other 14 major allergens.

Nutritional valuesWhole recipe (300 g pastry + 90 g butter + 1.4 kg apples + 100 g sugar, excluding the crème fraîche to serve): ~2,982 kcal, ~19 g protein, ~405 g carbohydrate, ~148 g fat — that is, per serving (6 people) about 497 kcal, 3 g protein, 68 g carbohydrate, 25 g fat.

Dietary suitabilityVegetarian, halal and kosher.

Variations (pears, mangoes, more butter or sugar, puff pastry): same allergens and dietary suitability, with nutritional values adjusted to the fruit and the quantities.

Serves 6Preparation 25 minutesCooking 15 minutes

Ingredients

  • 250 g strawberries, cut lengthways into 3
  • 50 g redcurrant and raspberry jelly
  • 50 ml kirsch
  • 1 beaten egg
  • 250 g crème pâtissière flavoured with kirsch
  • (or 250 g Chantilly cream flavoured with vanilla sugar)
  • 300 g sweet shortcrust pastry
  • Mint, verbena or acacia flowers, to decorate

Utensils

  • A tart tin
  • A rolling pin
  • Baking parchment
  • A spoon or spatula
  • A cooling rack
  • A bowl for the jelly

Method

Preheat the oven to 180 °C. Roll out the pastry and line the tart tin with it. Cover the pastry with baking parchment, then spread over a good layer of dried beans to stop it rising. Crimp the edges and bake blind for 10 minutes. Remove the parchment and the beans. Brush the tart base with beaten egg and return it to the oven for 5 minutes.

Unmould the pastry case and leave it to cool on a rack.

In a saucepan, melt the redcurrant and raspberry jelly, then let it cool to lukewarm. Now add the kirsch.

When the pastry case is cold, spread the base with a layer of crème pâtissière or Chantilly cream. Arrange the strawberries in concentric circles, starting from the outside. Coat them with the jelly. Decorate with mint, shredded verbena or acacia flowers.

AllergensGluten (sweet shortcrust pastry) — Milk (butter in the pastry, crème pâtissière) — Eggs (egg wash, crème pâtissière) — Sulphites and alcohol (kirsch) — none of the other 14 major allergens.

Nutritional valuesWhole recipe (250 g strawberries + 50 g jelly + 50 ml kirsch + 250 g crème pâtissière + 300 g sweet shortcrust pastry + egg wash): ~2,250 kcal, ~26 g protein, ~143 g carbohydrate, ~129 g fat — that is, per serving (6 people) about 375 kcal, 4 g protein, 24 g carbohydrate, 21 g fat.

Dietary suitabilityVegetarian and kosher, subject to the certification of the kirsch.
